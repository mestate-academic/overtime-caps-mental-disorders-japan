## ABSTRACT

**Objectives** Japan capped overtime in two steps: most industries from April 2019 (large firms) or April 2020 (small and medium-sized firms), and construction and motor-vehicle driving, previously exempt, from April 2024. Within 10 years, this created two natural experiments. We examined whether either cap was followed by fewer compensated work-related mental disorders.

**Methods** Using national workers' compensation data for fiscal years (FY) 2016–2025 (April 2016–March 2026), we compared approvals and claims for mental disorders per million employed persons between construction and transport (exempt until 2024) and six comparison industries, using Poisson difference-in-differences and event-study models. Experiment 1 (April 2019/2020 cap) compared FY2016–2018 with FY2019–2023; experiment 2 (April 2024 cap) compared FY2021–2023 with FY2024–2025. Cerebro-cardiovascular disease, recognised mainly on the basis of long hours, served as a positive control for exposure: it should decline if the caps reduced extreme hours. We added sensitivity, placebo and prefecture-level analyses and grouped approvals by precipitating event.

**Results** Approvals more than doubled over the decade (498 to 1082). Neither cap was followed by a relative decline in the newly capped industries (experiment 1: incidence rate ratio [IRR] 1.07, 95% CI 0.92 to 1.24; experiment 2: 1.10, 0.93 to 1.30); declines larger than 8% were excluded. Approvals in construction and transport rose sharply in FY2023, before enforcement; excluding that year, the IRRs were 1.19 (1.03 to 1.36) and 1.30 (1.11 to 1.53). Cerebro-cardiovascular approvals did not decline after either cap (1.01, 0.80 to 1.28; 0.96, 0.70 to 1.32). Harassment rose from 21% to 50% of approvals, whereas working-hour events fell from 17% to 6%.

**Conclusions** In two natural experiments over 10 years, statutory overtime caps were not followed by fewer compensated mental disorders. Because compliance and unrecorded overtime could not be measured, this does not show that reducing actual working hours is ineffective.

## KEY MESSAGES

**What is already known on this topic**
- Long working hours are associated with depression and suicide; Japan's overtime caps, in force from 2019, reduced paid overtime by about a quarter, but some men shifted to unpaid overtime.
- Whether statutory overtime caps reduce work-related mental disorders at population level is unknown.

**What this study adds**
- Japan's two-step introduction of overtime caps provided two natural experiments within 10 years of national data; in neither were compensated mental disorders lower after the cap, and declines larger than 8% were excluded.
- Cerebro-cardiovascular disease, the outcome most directly tied to long hours, also did not decline; the doubling of compensated mental disorders was driven by harassment, not working hours.

**How this study might affect research, practice or policy**
- Compensation statistics alone cannot show whether working-time policy works; monitoring should include measured hours, including unrecorded overtime.
- Prevention should also target harassment by supervisors and customers; these results do not support relaxing the caps.

## INTRODUCTION

Long working hours are associated with depression, anxiety and suicide,¹⁻³ and the WHO/ILO Joint Estimates attributed 745 000 deaths from stroke and ischaemic heart disease in 2016 to working 55 hours or more per week.¹ Japan had no binding legal limit on overtime until the Work Style Reform Act of 2018.⁴ The Act capped overtime at 45 hours a month and 360 hours a year (up to 720 hours a year and 100 hours a month in exceptional cases), with penalties for violations. It applied to large firms from April 2019 and to small and medium-sized firms from April 2020; construction, motor-vehicle driving and physicians were exempt until April 2024 (the "2024 problem").⁵

The caps reduced long working hours, paid overtime by about 25%,⁶ ⁷ but some men replaced paid with unpaid overtime and gained no well-being.⁶ Evaluations so far, including the government's own,⁸ have measured working hours, wages and self-reported well-being; whether the caps reduced work-related mental disorders is unknown. The workers' compensation system offers an annual measure: the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare (MHLW) publishes claims and approvals for mental disorders by industry, prefecture and precipitating event.⁹ Approvals have risen every year since FY2018 and exceeded 1000 in FY2024.⁹

The two-step introduction created two natural experiments within one decade (figure 1). From April 2019/2020, six industries were capped while construction and transport were not; from April 2024, construction and transport were capped while the six already were. Because compensation data also respond to the criteria for recognising mental disorders as work-related—revised in September 2023 to list customer harassment as a precipitating event¹⁰—and to claiming behaviour, we also analysed cerebro-cardiovascular disease, for which long working hours are the main recognition criterion, as a positive control for exposure: it should fall if the caps reduced extreme hours, and it shares the claiming environment of mental disorders. We asked whether either cap was followed by fewer compensated mental disorders in the newly capped industries.

## METHODS

### Design and data
We conducted a quasi-experimental study using publicly available aggregate data for the 10 fiscal years FY2016–2025 (April 2016 to March 2026), reported according to STROBE.¹¹ From the MHLW annual reports⁹—each lists the current and the previous year, and the FY2025 report was published in July 2026—we transcribed, by industry, claims and approvals for mental disorders and for cerebro-cardiovascular diseases, and national approvals by precipitating event. Overlapping years were cross-checked, and approvals were also checked against a ministry compilation.¹² Prefecture counts are published from FY2021. All transcribed tables summed to the published national totals (supplementary table S1). Denominators were annual average employed persons by industry from the Labour Force Survey¹³ and, for prefectures, the 2015 Population Census.¹⁴

### Two natural experiments
The exempt group comprised construction and transport and postal services (which includes motor-vehicle driving). The comparison group comprised the six other industries reported separately throughout the period with consistent denominators: manufacturing; wholesale and retail; finance and insurance; education; information and communication; and accommodation and food services. Health care and welfare was capped in two steps—most workers from April 2019/2020 but physicians (about 4% of its workers) only from April 2024, with higher limits—so it fits neither group and is shown for reference only; the remaining divisions form a residual category and were not used. Because each cap took effect on 1 April, the first day of a fiscal year, every fiscal year lies wholly before or after each cap.

- **Experiment 1 (April 2019/2020 cap):** the six comparison industries were newly capped (large firms from April 2019, small and medium-sized firms from April 2020) and construction and transport served as controls; FY2016–2018 (before) was compared with FY2019–2023 (after), FY2019 being a transition year in which only large firms were capped.
- **Experiment 2 (April 2024 cap):** construction and transport were newly capped and the six industries served as controls; FY2021–2023 was compared with FY2024–2025.

The two experiments were estimated separately to avoid comparisons with already-treated groups.¹⁵

### Statistical analysis
We fitted Poisson regressions of annual counts with industry and year fixed effects, an interaction between the newly capped group and the post-cap period, log employed persons as offset, and heteroskedasticity-robust (HC1) standard errors, which also accommodate overdispersion. The primary outcome was approved claims for mental disorders; secondary outcomes were claims for mental disorders and approvals and claims for cerebro-cardiovascular diseases, which served as a positive control for exposure: long working hours are their main recognition criterion, so they should decline if the caps reduced extreme hours, whereas changes in claiming behaviour common to both diseases would move them together. For each experiment, an event study interacted the newly capped group with each year, relative to the mean (on the log scale) of that experiment's pre-cap period (FY2016–2018 or FY2021–2023), so that every year has an estimate and a CI; experiment 2's event study began in FY2020, the first year in which all six control industries were capped. Single reference years (FY2022, FY2023) were used in sensitivity analyses. The IRR from this model is the ratio of the after/before rate ratio in the newly capped group to that in the control group, so the control group's change stands in for what would have happened without the cap; this requires that, without the cap, the two groups would have followed parallel trends, which we examined with the pre-cap event-study estimates. For each estimate we report the relative decline that the lower 95% confidence limit rules out.

Because there are only nine industries, we also ran a permutation test for experiment 2, re-estimating the model with each of the 28 pairs of the eight industries as placebo-treated. Prespecified sensitivity analyses excluded FY2023 (anticipation), FY2020–2021 (COVID-19) and accommodation and food services, extended experiment 2's pre-period to FY2016, and analysed construction and transport separately. Post hoc analyses excluded the FY2019 transition year from experiment 1, added health care and welfare to the newly capped group in experiment 2, and used FY2019–2023 or FY2020–2023 as its pre-period. A prefecture panel (47 prefectures, FY2021–2025) related the 2015 share of employment in construction and transport (standardised) to approvals after 2024, with prefecture and year fixed effects and standard errors clustered by prefecture. Approvals by precipitating event were grouped into harassment, change in workload, working hours (≥80 hours of overtime in a month or ≥2 weeks of continuous work), accidents and injury, special events and other. Analyses used Python 3 and statsmodels 0.15; data and code are available at https://github.com/hironoriuematsu-byte/overtime-caps-mental-disorders-japan.

### Patient and public involvement
Patients and the public were not involved.

### Ethics
Only publicly available aggregate statistics without information on identifiable individuals were used. Such research falls outside the scope of the Japanese Ethical Guidelines for Medical and Biological Research Involving Human Subjects,¹⁶ and ethical review was not required.

## RESULTS

### Ten-year trends
Over the 10 years, approvals for mental disorders more than doubled, from 498 in FY2016 to 1082 in FY2025, and rose in every industry group (figure 1; table 1). The approval rate rose from 7.2 to 12.6 per million employed persons in the six comparison industries, from 11.9 to 24.5 in construction and transport, and from 9.9 to 30.8 in health care and welfare. Claims rose faster (24.0 to 64.2, 33.9 to 80.8 and 37.4 to 136.0 per million). Cerebro-cardiovascular approvals did not rise (table 1).

### Experiment 1: the April 2019/2020 cap
Approvals in the newly capped industries did not decline relative to construction and transport (IRR 1.07, 95% CI 0.92 to 1.24; table 2), ruling out declines larger than 8%. Claims rose (1.12, 1.02 to 1.23). Cerebro-cardiovascular approvals (1.01, 0.80 to 1.28) and claims (0.96, 0.85 to 1.08) did not change, ruling out declines larger than 20% and 15%. In the event study (figure 2A), the pre-cap estimates for FY2016–2018 were close to 1 (0.94 to 1.04), consistent with parallel trends; approvals in the newly capped industries were not lower than before the cap in any year from FY2019 to FY2022 (IRRs 1.06 to 1.27); the IRR of 0.79 (0.68 to 0.92) in FY2023 reflects the rise in construction and transport that year. Cerebro-cardiovascular approvals did not change in any year (0.93 to 1.14).

### Experiment 2: the April 2024 cap
Approvals in construction and transport did not decline relative to the six comparison industries (IRR 1.10, 0.93 to 1.30), ruling out declines larger than 7%. Claims changed little (0.94, 0.87 to 1.03), nor did cerebro-cardiovascular approvals (0.96, 0.70 to 1.32) or claims (0.98, 0.88 to 1.08). In the event study (figure 2B), the relative rate in construction and transport was stable in FY2020–2022 (IRRs 0.87 to 0.99 relative to the FY2021–2023 mean), consistent with parallel trends until it rose in FY2023, the year before the cap (1.32, 1.20 to 1.46), and did not fall below the pre-cap level in FY2024 (1.08, 0.96 to 1.23) or FY2025 (1.18, 0.99 to 1.41) (supplementary table S2). With FY2022 as a single reference year, the IRRs were 1.53 (1.31 to 1.78) in FY2023, 1.25 (1.05 to 1.49) in FY2024 and 1.37 (1.14 to 1.64) in FY2025. Cerebro-cardiovascular approvals did not change in any year (0.85 to 1.07).

### Robustness
Excluding FY2023, the IRRs were 1.19 (1.03 to 1.36) in experiment 1 and 1.30 (1.11 to 1.53) in experiment 2. Other sensitivity analyses gave IRRs of 1.03 to 1.17 (supplementary table S3); excluding the FY2019 transition year, experiment 1 gave 1.03 (0.88 to 1.21); in experiment 2, adding health care and welfare to the newly capped group gave 1.13 (1.02 to 1.26), and using FY2019–2023 or FY2020–2023 as the pre-period gave 1.15 (1.00 to 1.33) and 1.11 (0.96 to 1.29). The experiment 2 estimate lay in the middle of the placebo distribution (0.79 to 1.15; permutation p=0.36). In the prefecture panel, the exposure ranged from 9.7% (Tokyo) to 16.5% (Miyagi), and the IRR per SD was 1.03 (0.95 to 1.12) for approvals and 0.99 (0.96 to 1.01) for claims (supplementary table S4).

### Precipitating events
Over the decade, harassment rose from 21% to 50% of approvals, whereas working-hour events fell from 17% to 6% and did not increase in number (86 in FY2016, 70 in FY2025) (table 3). Of the 346 additional approvals between FY2022 and FY2024, 195 (56%) involved harassment—108 customer harassment, recognised from September 2023, and 77 power harassment by supervisors—and 28 (8%) working hours.

## DISCUSSION

### Principal findings
Japan's two-step introduction of overtime caps provided two natural experiments within 10 years of national data. In neither experiment were compensated mental disorders lower in the newly capped industries after the cap. Estimates were close to 1, ruled out declines larger than about 8%, and were robust to sensitivity, placebo and prefecture-level analyses. Instead, recognition in construction and transport rose before the April 2024 cap, and the doubling of compensated cases was driven by harassment, not working hours. Cerebro-cardiovascular disease, the outcome most directly tied to long hours, did not decline after either cap.

### Were the caps complied with?
Our exposure was the legal cap, not the hours actually worked, and compliance was incomplete. In FY2024, inspectors visiting 26 512 workplaces suspected of long hours found illegal overtime in 42.4%; in these, the longest-working employee exceeded 80 hours of overtime in a month in 48.7% and 100 hours in 28.4%. Working time was inadequately recorded in 15.1%, and unpaid overtime was found in 8.0%.¹⁷ In 2024, labour inspection offices handled 22 354 cases of unpaid wages affecting 185 197 workers.¹⁸ These workplaces are not representative, but hours reported by households have long exceeded those reported by employers by about 300 hours per worker per year, a gap usually attributed to unpaid overtime,¹⁹⁻²¹ and after the April 2019 cap some men replaced paid with unpaid overtime.⁶ Managers, workers under discretionary schemes and physicians (up to 1860 hours a year) were partly or wholly outside the caps.

If the caps reduced recorded rather than actual hours—especially among the few workers with the longest hours, who generate most claims—the true change in exposure was much smaller than the legal change, and our estimates are biased towards the null. The positive control supports this. Had extreme hours fallen substantially, cerebro-cardiovascular claims should have fallen; yet after the April 2019/2020 cap, which covered more than 30 million workers, declines larger than 15–20% were ruled out. Our findings therefore concern the legal caps, not the effect of reducing actual hours. Studies that make actual working time visible—for example, by estimating unrecorded overtime by industry from household and employer surveys, or by using electronic records of computer use and building entry—could overturn these findings and are needed.

### Other explanations
Three further mechanisms may contribute. First, working-hour events accounted for only 15–18% of approvals before the caps; most cases follow harassment, changes in workload or accidents, which the caps do not address. That hours-related approvals did not grow while total approvals doubled is compatible with a modest effect of the caps on such cases, but this nationwide effect cannot be identified here and was far smaller than the growth in other events. Second, regulation may raise awareness of compensation rights: claims rose in the newly capped industries after the April 2019/2020 cap, and approvals in construction and transport rose in FY2023, amid intense media coverage but before the cap applied. Third, the September 2023 revision of recognition criteria raised approvals in all industries.¹⁰

### Comparison with other studies
Earlier evaluations found fewer paid overtime hours with partial substitution by unpaid overtime⁶ and better self-rated health,⁷ declining long hours in construction and logistics before 2024,⁵ and a levelling-off of the decline in suicide among employed men around the time of the reform.²² We add that these changes have not, so far, reduced compensated mental disorders. The predominance of interpersonal events agrees with analyses of Japanese compensation data²³ and with meta-analyses linking workplace bullying to depression.²⁴ ²⁵ Japan's harassment-prevention law, which required employers to prevent power harassment from June 2020 (large firms) and April 2022 (small and medium-sized firms), reduced self-reported harassment by about 7%,²⁶ but its effect on harassment-related disorders is unknown. Customer harassment has been a compensable event since September 2023, but employers were not required to prevent it until 1 October 2026 (FY2026), after our study period;²⁷ compensation data from FY2027 will be the first to reflect that duty.

### Strengths and limitations
Strengths are complete national data over 10 years, two natural experiments in which the roles of the groups reverse, employment denominators, a positive control for exposure in both experiments, and prespecified sensitivity and placebo analyses. There are limitations. First, exposure was the legal cap, not measured hours. Second, the design is ecological with nine industries; robust standard errors may be unreliable with few units (hence the permutation test); the exempt divisions include many workers who were never exempt, diluting exposure; and the April 2019/2020 cap reached small and medium-sized firms a year after large firms, which industry data cannot separate. Third, experiment 1 overlapped with the COVID-19 pandemic; excluding pandemic years or accommodation and food services did not change the results, but other industry-specific shocks cannot be excluded. Fourth, experiment 2 has only two post-cap years, and decisions lag claims by months. Fifth, compensation data capture only claimed and recognised cases; the recognition criteria for mental disorders were revised in May 2020 and September 2023¹⁰ and those for cerebro-cardiovascular disease in September 2021,²⁸ all nationwide and therefore absorbed by year fixed effects unless their effect differed by industry. Sixth, prefecture-level exposure varied little (SD 1.3 percentage points). Seventh, denominators were all employed persons, including the self-employed, who are not covered by the compensation insurance and are numerous in construction; this understates rates but, being stable over time, should not bias the difference-in-differences estimates. Denominators were not adjusted for age or sex.

### Implications
Preventing work-related mental disorders in Japan requires action on psychosocial hazards—above all harassment by supervisors and customers—alongside working-time regulation, and enforcement should target the recording of actual hours. Compensation statistics, whose annual records are widely reported, should not be used alone to judge working-time policy. This matters now: in October 2025, as the statutory five-year review of the Act began, the government instructed the ministry to consider relaxing working-time regulation.²⁹ The results do not support relaxing the caps: the absence of any fall in cerebro-cardiovascular claims suggests that exposure changed less than the law implies, not that the caps are unnecessary. Linking compensation records to measured hours, and extending follow-up beyond FY2025, would allow a more definitive evaluation.

## CONCLUSION
Across two natural experiments in 10 years of national data, Japan's statutory overtime caps were not followed by fewer compensated mental disorders, and the doubling of compensated cases was driven by harassment rather than working hours. Whether the caps reduced actual, rather than recorded, working time remains unknown; studies that make unrecorded overtime visible may change these conclusions.
